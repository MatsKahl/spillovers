# Cross-Border Competition and Tax Pass-Through: Evidence from Germany's 2022 Fuel Tax Cut

**Mats Petter Kahl**, Universidad Carlos III de Madrid

[Working paper (PDF)](Kahl_Cross_Border_Competition.pdf), October 2026

**Abstract.** How does a national tax cut pass through at an international border when one side is treated, and the other is not? I study Germany's 2022 fuel-tax cut using station-level price data and exact driving distances to French competitors. German diesel stations within 10 km of a French gas station cut prices by 0.96 cents per liter more than stations further than 50 km inland. That corresponds to a pass-through premium of about 6 percentage points. The premium appears on the first day of the cut, is largest within a few kilometers of the border, and disappears after the tax returns. The premium arises where the tax cut closed the cross-border price gap, that is, for diesel, and it doesn't appear where the gap stayed wide, that is, for gasoline. A model with treated domestic and untreated foreign rivals shows that, at a given intensity of cross-border substitution, feedback through foreign prices explains at most a small part of the premium. The evidence is consistent with a change in that intensity: cross-border competition that becomes active when the price gap is small. The incidence of a national tax cut therefore varies within a country with local competitive conditions, here, at the border.
