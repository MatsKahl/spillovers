# Cross-Border Competition and Tax Pass-Through: Evidence from Germany's 2022 Fuel Tax Cut

**Mats Petter Kahl**, Universidad Carlos III de Madrid

[Working paper (PDF)](Kahl_Cross_Border_Competition.pdf), November 2026

**Abstract.** How does a national tax cut pass through at an international border on the treated side? How does it spill over to the untreated side? I study Germany's 2022 fuel-tax cut using station-level price data and exact driving distances to French competitors. German diesel stations within 10 km of a French gas station cut prices by 0.96 cents per liter (95% confidence interval: 0.43–1.49) more than stations further than 50 km inland. That corresponds to a pass-through premium of about 6 percentage points (95% confidence interval: 2.6–8.9). The premium appears at the start of the cut, is largest within a few kilometers of the border, and disappears after the tax returns. French stations near the border also cut prices by a similar amount, but they were already falling relative to inland France before the cut.

The premium arises where the tax cut closed the cross-border price gap, that is, for diesel, and it does not appear where the gap stayed wide, that is, for gasoline. A model with treated domestic and untreated foreign rivals shows that, at a given intensity of cross-border substitution, feedback through foreign prices cannot account for the premium on its own. The evidence is consistent with a change in that intensity: cross-border competition that becomes active when the price gap is small. The incidence of a national tax cut varies within a country with local competitive conditions.
